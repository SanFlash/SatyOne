# SatyOne — Legal and Responsible Use Policy

Status: Product policy baseline; qualified legal review required before public launch. Last reviewed: 12 November 2026.

> This is product guidance, not legal advice. Applicability depends on the operator, users, data, processing, jurisdictions, contracts and launch date. Laws and enforcement dates change.

## 1. Core rule
Use SatyOne only for lawful, authorized, non-deceptive and rights-respecting purposes. The user remains responsible for the systems they access, the data they submit, the outputs they use and the actions they take. A prompt, API key, public URL or technical ability does not create legal permission.

## 2. Disallowed use
SatyOne content and features must not facilitate:
- Unauthorized access, account takeover, credential stuffing, password theft, phishing or impersonation.
- Malware, ransomware, botnets, destructive payloads, stealth, persistence, evasion or command-and-control.
- Exfiltration, doxxing, stalking, non-consensual tracking, spyware or covert surveillance.
- Denial-of-service, destructive testing or intentional disruption of real systems.
- Circumvention of access controls, licensing, DRM, provider safeguards or contractual limits.
- Fraud, scams, identity theft, forged evidence or deceptive impersonation.
- Non-consensual sexual content, exploitation or targeting of minors.
- Infringement of copyright, trademarks, patents, trade secrets, privacy or publicity/personality rights.
- Discrimination, harassment, threats or other unlawful conduct.

For legitimate dual-use security education, focus on owned labs, synthetic data, defensive explanation, remediation and non-destructive validation.

## 3. Authorization for security testing
Before testing a system, account, network, device or dataset:
- Confirm ownership or written authorization from its owner.
- Define exact domains, IP ranges, accounts, environments and time window.
- Define permitted/prohibited techniques, rate limits, test source IPs and escalation contacts.
- Explicitly address whether scanning, credential testing, exploitation, social engineering or data access are excluded.
- Use test accounts and synthetic data where possible.
- Stop if an asset is out of scope, real personal data is exposed, instability occurs or scope is exceeded.
- Follow the current rules and safe-harbor language of any bug bounty or disclosure program. General permission to report bugs is not blanket permission to test all assets.

## 4. Privacy and personal data
Apply data minimization. Request only data needed for the task. Prefer synthetic, anonymized or redacted examples. Warn users not to paste passwords, API keys, access tokens, private keys, payment data, government identifiers, health records or confidential employer/customer data into unapproved workflows.

Before collecting personal data, define purpose, applicable legal basis/consent, notice, retention, deletion, access, security and complaint handling. Disclose third-party providers and subprocessors. Assess cross-border transfers and data residency. Do not sell personal data or repurpose it without a valid basis.

### India
For an India-facing service, assess the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025, including official commencement notifications, amendments and corrigenda. Commencement is phased; do not assume every substantive obligation began on Gazette publication. Obtain legal advice on whether the operator is a Data Fiduciary or Data Processor for each activity and on notices, consent, rights, grievance handling, safeguards, breach response, retention, children's data, processors and transfers.

Official MeitY reference: https://www.meity.gov.in/documents/act-and-policies/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-gDOxUjMtQWa

### Other jurisdictions
Assess GDPR/ePrivacy in the EU/EEA, UK GDPR, US state and sector-specific privacy laws, Canada's federal/provincial laws, Brazil LGPD, Singapore PDPA and other relevant laws. This list is not exhaustive; applicability depends on the actual service and data flows.

## 5. Cyber incident obligations
Maintain incident detection, triage, containment, evidence preservation, notification assessment, remediation and post-incident review. For India operations, assess applicable CERT-In directions under section 70B of the Information Technology Act, 2000, including reporting timelines, log retention and time synchronization. Confirm current applicability and deadlines with counsel and official guidance.

Official reference: https://www.cert-in.org.in/Directions70B.jsp

## 6. Intellectual property and provider terms
Respect copyright, trademarks, patents, trade secrets, database rights, licences, publicity/personality rights and provider terms. Do not scrape, copy or republish content where prohibited. Use original summaries and links to authoritative sources. Track licences and attribution for open-source software, datasets and examples. Do not assume AI output is free of third-party rights or automatically protectable. Provide a rights/takedown contact process.

For each provider adapter, review current API terms, acceptable-use policy, privacy policy, pricing, rate limits, regional availability and data-processing terms. Use documented APIs; do not bypass authentication, quotas, billing or safety controls. Keep credentials server-side and disclose when user data is sent to a provider.

## 7. High-impact and regulated decisions
SatyOne is not a substitute for qualified professionals. For legal, medical, mental-health, financial, insurance, employment, housing, education, credit or public-benefit decisions, state limitations and uncertainty, require appropriate human review, and do not present generated output as a binding determination or diagnosis. Avoid unlawful discrimination and unsupported automated consequential decisions.

## 8. Transparency
Never claim “100% legal”, “fully compliant worldwide”, “unhackable”, “hallucination-free” or “guaranteed secure”. Do not claim a model, provider integration, benchmark, price or capability without current evidence. Label educational templates, unverified content, provider terms, human-review requirements and last-checked dates.

## 9. Reporting and enforcement
Provide a contact route for privacy requests, complaints, vulnerability reports, rights complaints and abuse reports. Acknowledge, triage, investigate, resolve and document reports. Restrict access to evidence and preserve only what is needed.

## 10. Public launch legal checklist
Obtain qualified legal review of operator identity, terms, privacy notice, cookies/analytics, data inventory, purposes, consent/legal basis, retention, processor contracts, subprocessors, incident response, rights/grievance process, children's access, takedown process, provider terms, export/deletion, backups and target jurisdictions.

No document alone makes SatyOne legally compliant. Compliance is an ongoing operational program.
